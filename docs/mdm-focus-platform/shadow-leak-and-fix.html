Your version, the port, and the leak

Two questions, one page: how your "keep the number on the phone" idea compares to porting, and — the sharper one — if the shadow line is a real dialable number, can't the student just hand it out and bypass everything? Yes, they can, unless the shadow line has no dialable number to give. Here's the whole picture.

The fix — a data-only shadow line, app as the only phone

  • Leak closed on both platforms: no voice number on the SIM means outbound can only go through your app (public # as caller ID) and there's no shadow number for anyone to call.
  • This is required on iPhone, where you can't reroute outbound or screen inbound — data-only removes the problem at its root instead of trying to patch it on-device.
  • Cost of the fix: you're now building a softphone (in-app VoIP calling/texting), call quality rides on data, and native Phone/Messages go away (which lines up with the MDM app-hiding anyway).
  • Do not hand-wave 911: data-only lines may not support native emergency dialing — solve it with E911-registered VoIP and/or a verified native fallback, with telecom/regulatory guidance.

Recommendation: keep the port (it's the only design that controls both calls and texts on iPhone), but make the shadow line data-only and your app the only dialer and messenger. That closes the exact bypass you spotted — with no dialable shadow number, there is nothing to hand out and nothing to call around, identically on both platforms.

Phasing: a voice-capable shadow line is fine for the MVP and for testing the routing (on Android it's already well-contained by the device-owner agent). Move to data-only + in-app calling for the shipping product, because bypass-resistance is the entire point of the platform — and solve emergency calling as a first-class requirement before launch.
